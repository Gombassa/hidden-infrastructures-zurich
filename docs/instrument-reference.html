Instrument Reference

Ten HTML control surfaces, each hosting one or more of the project's sonic behaviours as playable Web Audio instruments. They exist to settle by ear what cannot be settled by argument — allocation policy, mapping curves, whether two layers stay distinguishable. Every parameter that carries a decision is exposed, and the defaults are the current build's values wherever those are known, so any change you make is a change you can hear against what already exists.

01Using the surfaces

All ten share the same conventions. Audio never starts without a gesture, so the first click is always Enable audio. Space bar triggers the surface's main action — spawn, walk, or fire. Nothing persists: reloading returns every control to its default, which is deliberate, because the defaults are the reference point.

Reading the defaults

Where the current build has a documented value, that value is the default. Where a parameter is new, the default is a proposal and is flagged as such in the panel hint beneath it. Panel hints carry the reasoning; they are not decoration and are the fastest way to know why a control exists.

Simulation vs GPS

Two surfaces read real position. The remaining eight simulate it — distance sliders, a walk animation, or a draggable plan. Simulation is not a lesser mode: it lets you hold one variable still, which street testing cannot. The two are complementary and the GPS-wired pair should be walked, not desk-tested.

Field caution Two surfaces fetch GeoJSON directly rather than through ProximityEngine, because that parser discards _dedupKey and the semantic properties. On mobile data those files are large and growing. Time the first load standing still before setting off.

02Tram electrical

The layer with the most behaviours and the only one already field-validated. All three of its sonic behaviours now exist as surfaces, so the layer can be judged as a whole rather than in parts.

Hiss Voice

GPS-wired hiss-voice.html

One voice per feeder in range, built on entry and destroyed on exit. Replaces the fixed 6-slot pool. The seed is hashed from the feeder's _dedupKey, so a given feeder always sounds like itself — Rennweg is recognisable on a second walk.

ParameterDefaultWhat it does, and why
Comb delay2.3–8.9 msSets the resonant series, 112–435 Hz. The old table fixed six values; a hashed draw across the same range gives a continuum, one point of which belongs permanently to each feeder.
LFO 1 → delay0.08–0.42 HzPitch and resonance drift. Rates are scaled to voice lifetime: a feeder inside 50 m lasts 20–70 s at walking pace, so anything below 0.05 Hz never completes a cycle.
LFO 2 → cutoff0.05–0.31 HzBreathing. Drawn separately from LFO 1 so the two phase against each other within a voice as well as across voices.
Rectifier tone600 Hz50 Hz mains through 12-pulse rectification. Per-voice detune means two voices beat rather than summing — set detune to 0 to hear the difference.
Falloff exp1.0Linear, matching the current build. 2.0 is the crackle's validated curve. This control is the mapping-curve audit.
At capmargin 20%Steal-furthest reproduces today's continuous slot reassignment. Margin adds hysteresis so GPS jitter doesn't churn voices. Refuse leaves sounding voices untouched.

Feeder Crackle

Simulation feeder-crackle.html

The only behaviour triggered by an external live feed rather than by your own position. A tram passes a feeder; you hear it at whatever gain your own distance gives. The route strip and falloff plot show both at once.

ParameterDefaultWhat it does, and why
Radius / exponent150 m / 2.0The validated (1−t)² curve. The draw effect comes from removing the listener gate entirely, so distant events stay faintly audible instead of hard-cutting.
Grain count5–14Contact arcing is a burst of micro-impulses, not one transient. Set count to 1 and window to 8 ms for the single-burst version.
Clump1.8Above 1 front-loads grains so the event has attack and tail rather than an even scatter.
Ring / surge430 Hz / 72 HzCable resonance and current draw. Both to zero gives a dry contact crackle — worth hearing at 100 m to check the event still carries without body.
ModeOn footOn tram, every feeder fires at zero distance and the draw disappears. Production detects this from GPS speed at about 3 m/s.
Note The only surface that creates nodes per event rather than per voice — three per grain. The readout tracks peak count; at real District 1 feeder density this rate will be far higher than the corridor simulates.

Powerline Drone

Simulation powerline-drone.html

Gated on proximity to the trasse line rather than to points, in a 15 m window, and modulated by the slowest LFOs in the project. The instrument exists to expose a mismatch between those two facts.

ParameterDefaultWhat it does, and why
Fundamental110 HzA2 exactly — the one pitch in the project chosen musically rather than derived. That makes this drone a tonal centre by default.
LFO 1 / 20.017 / 0.023 Hz59 s and 43 s periods, one per oscillator so the beat rate itself drifts. Deliberately incommensurate — they never realign.
Gate20 m → 5 mSilent beyond outer, full at inner. The tightest gate in the project.
Cycles heardreadoutEncounter duration ÷ LFO period. Below about 0.25 the sweep is not modulation — it is a random static offset, different every pass.
Private reverb2.0 s / 3%The only layer with a reverb of its own on top of the shared density bus. Both exposed so you can judge whether that exception earns itself.
Waiting on Walk along versus walk across. Same parameters, completely different results — along Bahnhofstrasse holds the gate open for minutes, crossing the tracks opens it for seconds.

03Water supply

The densest layer, and the one where a new behaviour was added. Mains are pressurised and permanently full, which is the property that separates water from sewage: pitched bore resonance against unpitched gravity flow.

Water Flow

GPS-wired New behaviour water-flow.html

A continuous bed that did not previously exist — water was the only entirely discrete layer. Adding it takes the inventory to 24 and puts water on the shared density reverb bus, which currently has five sends and a wet curve calibrated against that count.

ParameterDefaultWhat it does, and why
Resonance modeBankBank gives every pipe in range its own resonance weighted by proximity — the local network becomes a chord. Single tracks the nearest and glides. Fixed ignores pipe function entirely and is the A/B control.
Main / conn Hz185 / 640 HzTwo conditions, classified from pipeType rather than the seven diameterClass values. Correlates with bore but has not been verified against a diameter table — WVZ has not published one.
Cutoff far/near420 / 2600 HzOpens as you approach — distant pipes heard through more ground.
Wander0.07 HzModulates cutoff, not amplitude, so it doesn't read as tremolo. Fernwärme owns tremolo.
Pressure sub0.05A ceiling, not a starting point — the one control that collides with sewage outright. Set to 0 and check water still reads as pressurised.

Pulse & Drip

Simulation water-pulse-drip.html

A pulse is water hammer — the bore struck rather than flowing. A drip is one entrained bubble, Minnaert again but an order of magnitude smaller than sewage's.

ParameterDefaultWhat it does, and why
Pulse main / conn800 / 1200 HzThe documented values. They sit well above the flow instrument's 185/640 Hz, so pulse and drone currently read as different objects. Dropping them onto those figures makes them the same pipe.
Hysteresis12%Widens the exit radius so a feature hovering at the boundary under GPS jitter doesn't re-arm and fire on every wobble. Probably wants to be higher given 10–15 m urban error.
Cooldown30 sPer feature. The Armed readout counts features whose cooldown has expired — if that number is large the layer chatters as soon as you move.
Drip size1.6–4.2 mmMinnaert f ≈ 3.28/r gives roughly 4100–1560 Hz. The documented 2800 Hz corresponds to a 2.3 mm bubble, so the figure is physically consistent.
Rate curve1.6Above 1 holds the rate low until a cluster is genuinely dense, so ordinary streets stay sparse and a junction arrives as an event rather than a gradient.

04Sewage

Rumble, Junction Thud & Gurgle

Simulation sewage-rumble.html

Three behaviours on one surface, Step 5: rumble and junction thud are strict parity ports (src/instruments/sewage-rumble.js, sewage-junction-thud.js — thud was previously unbuilt). The gurgle is voiced by sewage-gurgle.js, which also drives the alongside loop on crossing-family.html — see below for why that's not the usual crossing/alongside pairing. The widest radius of any layer at 80 m, so in District 1 this is almost always present.

05Electricity

Transformer Hum

Simulation electricity-hum.html

The contract test. This is the first instrument that gates on one geometry — nodes — while being lifted by another — cables. The allocator from the hiss voice transferred to it unmodified, which is the strongest evidence so far that it is a reusable component rather than a per-instrument detail.

ParameterDefaultWhat it does, and why
Fundamental100 HzTwice mains — magnetostriction, the physical sound a transformer makes.
Emphasis k6–15Which harmonic carries most energy. k=15 lands on 1500 Hz, the current build's scream, but now as a harmonic of the real grid. k=2–3 is the boxy hum beside a transformer. Low frequencies couple badly out of a buried enclosure, which is why street level hears the upper series.
Even bias0.45Favours 200/400/600 Hz, which is what magnetostriction actually produces most of. The odd harmonics are the licence.
Beat1.4 HzTwo oscillators per voice. Real transformers are phase-locked to one grid, so this is licence — set to 0 for the strict version.
At cap → refusemarginRefuse new reproduces the current build exactly: first free slot wins, nothing on full. That was never chosen — it is what an 8-slot pool does by default.
Voltage modeHashedOff by default. voltageClass has never been read out of the GeoJSON, so voltage-driven emphasis is a hypothesis with a switch, not a mapping.

06Telecom

Telecom Burst, Chirp & Handshake

Simulation telecom-burst.html

Three behaviours, Step 6: burst pool (src/instruments/telecom-burst-pool.js), node-entry chirp (telecom-node-chirp.js — previously unbuilt), and node dwell handshake (telecom-node-handshake.js), all strict parity ports. The one pool confirmed to need no allocator — four fixed slots, nothing claimed per feature, gated collectively by aggregate proximity (nearest-cable distance, cable count). Also the layer with no acoustic original: fibre makes no sound, so this is where the piece stops sonifying and starts representing.

ParameterValueWhat it does, and why
Gate rate22–78 Hz, fixed sine LFOBelow pitch and above rhythm, where amplitude modulation reads as roughness rather than pulse. Density scales these 0.5–2.0×. This page's earlier Packets/Shape/Duty exploration (framed bursts, variable-duty gate) had no production counterpart and was retired once the real class landed — production's gate is a plain sine.
Node chirp2000→4000 Hz, 0.2sDebounced per node id until its own oscillator's onended fires — not a time cooldown. Fires on entry within 40m.
Dwell threshold5 sThe only time-based trigger in the project. Fires only if you stop near a node and stay stopped. Tracked caller-side (this page owns the dwell Map), not inside the handshake class — same precedent as feeder crackle's re-fire guard.
Handshake sweep1000→8000 Hz, 0.4s, 8s cooldownCooldown is internal to the class. No jitter in production — this page's earlier ±500Hz jitter exploration was its own addition, not carried into the port.
Open question, unchanged by Step 6 Nothing else in the piece rewards standing still. Whether the handshake is a good secret or simply undiscoverable is a design decision, not a parameter — and it may need a shorter threshold to be findable at all.

07Fernwärme

Thermal Drone

Simulation fernwaerme-thermal.html

The only directional behaviour — StereoPanner, not HRTF (HRTF localises poorly below ~200Hz, and this tone is 60Hz). Uses a draggable plan rather than distance sliders because direction and crossing cannot be judged parametrically. Voiced by the real FernwaermeThermal class now (Step 7): all three behaviours (tone, crossing burst, alongside loop) are one class sharing one panner, since production's burst deliberately inherits the tone's own bearing-driven pan rather than playing centred like every other layer's crossing sound. The detection geometry (canvas map, drag, heading, crossing/alongside math) is unchanged prototype code — this page's own copy of the shared cross-layer detection path, same as crossing-family.html's.

ParameterValueWhat it does, and why
Pansin(rel), fixed, 1.0s TCProduction's only mode. Folds front and back onto the same value — a pipe ahead and one behind both pan centre. This page's earlier linear/off/width exploration had no production counterpart and is retired.
TremoloCarrier, fixedMultiplicative on the carrier, not the master — production's anti-bleed routing. The earlier master-vs-carrier "bleed" demo toggle is retired now that the real class only implements the correct path.
Tone60 Hz sine, fixedNo partials, no flow noise — production is a bare sine. This page's earlier partials/flow-noise exploration (added because a pure tone is near-inaudible on bone-conduction headphones) is retired; worth revisiting as a real design question, just not inside the parity class.
GPS noise0 mAdds a random-walk offset at 1 Hz fix rate. At 10–15 m — realistic for urban canyons — the crossing test fires on movement you never made. Those are logged as CROSS? with both distances.
Detection ×40.5 m / 20 m / 35° / 3 sMin move, alongside radius, acute angle, crossing cooldown. These govern every LineString layer, not just this one — unchanged, still tunable, same treatment as crossing-family.html's own Detection panel.

08Crossing & alongside

Shared Event Family

Simulation crossing-family.html

Eight behaviours on one surface — four crossing transients and four alongside loops. Only electricity and water share one detection path and one voicing function (LineCrossingVoice) end-to-end; only frequency, Q, decay and gain differ between them. Sewage and telecom both broke that pattern, for different reasons. Sewage's (Step 5) is a pairing break: its alongside loop re-fires the gurgle sound instead of the crossing snap, so it's dispatched to a separate SewageGurgle instance. Telecom's (Step 6) is a synthesis break: crossing and alongside pair the same way electricity/water do, but the sound is a swept sine oscillator, which LineCrossingVoice's noise+bandpass _fire() can't produce — so both route to a separate TelecomClickVoice instance instead. See the parameter table below for current (parity) crossing values; the field-tuned session further down predates both splits and describes the old exploratory "thud"/"click" panels.

ParameterDefaultWhat it does, and why
Water knock380 Hz, Q 18Resonant, short — a struck pipe, lower than the entry pulse.
Sewage crossing200 Hz, Q 3.5Production's exact value (SEWAGE_CROSSING, Step 5) — the prototype's exploratory Q 6 is superseded. Alongside is a different sound entirely; see above.
Electricity snap2200 Hz, 0.06 sSharp, fully noise-based, almost no decay.
Telecom click3500 → 6000 Hz, 0.06 sProduction's exact value (telecom-click-voice.js, Step 6) — the prototype's exploratory noise/sine mix and independent Q are superseded; production is a pure sine sweep, no noise component at all.
Collisions150 ms windowTwo transients inside the window count as one collision. Turns amber past 25% — at that point they are arriving as composites and individual tuning will not fix it.
Stagger0 msDelays colliding events so they arrive in sequence rather than as a chord. A small lie about simultaneity in exchange for legibility.
Architectural finding, settled in Step 6 This family was expected to become one instrument with four presets, taking the inventory from 24 to about 17. It doesn't: only electricity and water (4 of the 8 rows) actually share LineCrossingVoice. Sewage and telecom each needed a class of their own, for different reasons (pairing vs. synthesis — see above). The consolidation was still worth doing where it held, just not the uniform outcome originally hoped for. The detection block here remains identical to the Fernwärme surface for all four layers regardless of which class voices them.

09Shared techniques

Patterns that recur and should become shared utilities rather than being reimplemented per instrument.

TechniqueUsed inWhy it exists
FNV-1a → mulberry32hiss, humFeature ID hashed to a 32-bit seed, seeding a deterministic RNG. Gives stable per-feature timbre and makes replay possible without storing parameters.
Phase-randomised LFOhiss, humOscillatorNode has no phase parameter. A PeriodicWave with phase-encoded coefficients gives arbitrary starting phase, so voices never move in lockstep.
Harmonic stack as one wavehumA 20-harmonic spectrum costs one oscillator instead of twenty. Rebuild and setPeriodicWave when the spectrum changes.
Variable-duty pulsetelecomFourier series of a pulse train as a PeriodicWave. The built-in square wave is fixed at 50% duty.
Allocatorhiss, humCap, policy, hysteresis margin, feature→voice map. Transferred between two structurally different instruments unmodified.
Minnaert bubblesewage, water dripf ≈ 3.28/r. One formula, two scales — centimetres for gurgle, millimetres for drips.
Random-walk modulationsewage, water flowIrregular amplitude drift where an LFO would read as tremolo. Keeps periodic modulation reserved for Fernwärme.
Jittered recursionsewage, fern, crossingsetTimeout rescheduling itself with a jittered interval. The documented pattern for alongside loops and gurgle.
Blocks integration parseLineFeatures() and parsePointFeatures() discard _dedupKey and the semantic properties, and assign a synthetic array-index id that is unstable across re-extraction. Two surfaces already work around this by fetching GeoJSON directly. That is fine for authoring and impossible in production, where instruments consume ProximityEngine output.

11Open decisions

Each of these is answerable by using a surface. None is answerable by argument.

  • Allocation policy — steal-furthest, margin, or refuse. Hiss and hum both expose all three. Needs street GPS at 25 m, not a slider.
  • Falloff exponent per layer — (1−t)² is validated on crackle only. Every other layer defaults to linear. The exponent control on each surface is the audit.
  • Water resonance mode — if Bank does not beat Fixed, the pipe-function mapping is not earning its complexity and should go.
  • Water pulse frequency — 800/1200 Hz as documented, or dropped onto 185/640 Hz so the pipe rings at the pitch it hums at.
  • Telecom packets — framed or continuous. If framing does not earn itself, the layer simplifies considerably.
  • Detection constants — one set governs all five line layers. Fernwärme and the crossing family must agree.
  • Stagger — whether to displace simultaneous transients for legibility, and by how much.
  • Dwell discoverability — whether the handshake is findable at a 5 s threshold, or at all.
  • Private reverb — whether the powerline drone's own reverb does something the shared bus cannot.
  • Density bus recalibration — the wet curve assumes five continuous sends. Water flow makes six.
  • Tonal centre — A2 is already established by the drone. The district theme either accepts it or works against it deliberately.
  • Control surfaces in production — still open, and currently being answered by default: the surfaces are reachable on the deployed URL and one is linked from index.html.

Behaviours remaining

All 24 behaviours are now built, and as of Step 7 (Fernwärme, the last per-layer step) all six layers are voiced by real production-parity classes rather than prototype-only synthesis. The two behaviours computed rather than driven by a given feature — sewage junction thud (Step 5, junctions clustered from pipe endpoints at 8 m) and telecom node-entry chirp (Step 6, structurally identical to the water entry pulse) — are both manual-test-only on their control surfaces, since neither's upstream computation (computeSewageJunctions(); node proximity is given directly for telecom, so this one was simpler than expected) is simulated in these prototypes. Remaining work is reintegration into index.html (Step 8), not further behaviour-building.

  1. Walk the two GPS-wired surfaces

    Hiss at 25 m first — that is production truth, and the allocation question needs street conditions. Then 50 m as the deliberate experiment. Water flow: Bank against Fixed, through the headphones you will actually use. Bring the log downloads back.

  2. Record the tuned values

    The parameter sets are the deliverable from this phase. Ten surfaces' worth is more than anyone reconstructs afterwards — screenshots of the panels are sufficient.

  3. Fix the ProximityEngine parser

    Carrying _dedupKey and the semantic property whitelist through the parse is a prerequisite for integration, not a nicety. Two instruments have already hit it independently.

  4. Regenerate the counts

    Three findings now show every documented figure is stale — tram at roughly 11,148 against 2,899, water at 10,568 against 3,339. The generator belongs inside the extraction pipeline, rewriting a marked region so it cannot drift again.

  5. Split into modules

    src/instruments/*.js for the audio cores plus the shared allocator; instruments/*.html importing them as control surfaces. One implementation, so tuning stays honest. Tuned values become the exported defaults.

  6. Integrate one layer behind a flag

    Tram first — its three behaviours are built and it is the field-validated layer. Keep the old pool code gated rather than deleted so both can be A/B'd on the same walk.

  7. Update the documents

    Inventory 24 not 23; the crossing family as one module; the allocator as a real component rather than a thin wrapper; the density bus at six sends; and the derivation table above, which the September application should be consistent with.

Sequencing note The structurally novel work is done. Every remaining mechanism has been proven at least once, so what is left is voicing, integration and field validation. That is a different kind of work and it benefits from being done a layer at a time, with the existing build audible alongside as a reference.
